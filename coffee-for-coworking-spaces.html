The coffee is the amenity members test on the tour.

People compare co-working spaces on three things they can judge in ten minutes: the desk, the meeting rooms and the coffee. The first two are capital decisions made once. The third is usually a small machine in a corner with a jar of instant beside it, and it is the one a member uses four times a day and thinks about when the renewal comes round.

What a Pod is, and what it is not.

A Pod is a compact coffee counter, 50 to 100 square feet, with no seating of its own. Your lounge is already the seating. What the Pod adds is a serious machine, specialty-grade beans ground at every order and someone trained to use both. Walk up, pick up, back to the desk, under ninety seconds.

It is not a cafe taking up a corner of your floor plan, and it is not a franchise you operate. It is a counter we run inside a space you run.

Without you running a cafe.

  • No baristas on your payroll, and no rota to cover when one is unwell.
  • No equipment to buy, service or depreciate.
  • No supply chain, no milk runs, no stock counts.
  • No food-and-beverage compliance work landing on your community manager.

You provide the space and the footfall. Everything behind the counter is ours.

It sits alongside your existing setup.

A Pod is the coffee counter, not the pantry and not the catering. It works alongside whatever meal, snack or event arrangement your space already runs, including a food-delivery or meal-card partner. If you already have a food vendor in the building, nothing about that has to change.

A reason to look up in month six.

Four drinks stay on the board: Black, White, Green and Strong. Behind them, a rotating set of versions that changes every few weeks. A hot desk is a monthly decision, and the difference between an amenity people use and one they stop noticing is whether anything about it ever changes.
