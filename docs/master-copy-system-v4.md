## IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

Read this section first if you are updating the website.

**What this document is.** Every word the brand speaks, final. Copy only. It says nothing about colour, layout, imagery or typography. The weightage tag on each block tells the designer how much visual presence the line should carry.

**What changed from v3, in one paragraph.** The brand no longer argues about price. Every Arabica claim is gone, replaced by a grade claim. The menu has split into two layers, The Letter and The P.S., and the range is now the lead differentiator. The voice has gained a second register for personality. The positioning line has arrived. Pod placement language has been corrected.

**Punctuation change.** The v3 convention `P.S. — line` becomes `P.S. line`, with no dash. Apply across every surface, including existing print files.

# PART 0 — POSITIONING AND RULES

## 0.1 The positioning

P.S. Coffee sells specialty coffee at a price that survives being a daily habit, in small pods placed in and around the buildings where people work, study and train.

The customer knows the difference between good coffee and bad, drinks it every working day, and has settled for the office machine or the chain because the specialty shop costs too much to repeat five times a week. They are not looking for an occasion. They want the same good cup, at the same time, without thinking about it, and they want a reason to still find it interesting in month six.

Three things to protect in every line: specialty quality, daily pricing, no ceremony. Anything working against the third is wrong for this brand, even when it sounds premium.

## 0.2 The positioning line

> We take coffee very seriously, as well as the drama.

**About page and landing page only, plus internal and B2B decks.** Not signage, not cups, not a repeated tagline. It is a statement someone reads once and understands the brand from. Repetition flattens it into a slogan.

Short form, internal and staff training: *We take coffee seriously. And the drama.*

## 0.3 What drama means

Two jobs, and both need stating because a writer will otherwise guess.

**Product drama.** Everyone else serves one version of each drink. We serve the original and several ways to have it, across the board, rotating. Range is what a chain cannot match at our price point and it is what keeps a daily habit from going stale.

**Copy drama.** Our customer spends eight hours surrounded by careful, hedged, approved language. Every other surface in that building was written by a committee. We are the one that was not.

Drama does not mean gossip about customers, emotional excess, or theatre at the counter. Nobody performs at anyone while they wait.

## 0.4 The letter and the postscript

Everything descends from one idea. A postscript is short, it comes after the main thing, and it is the part people actually read.

**The day is the letter. The coffee is the P.S.**

| The letter | The P.S. |
| --- | --- |
| The day, the building, the routine | The coffee, the interruption |
| The printed board, four drinks, never changes | The rotating versions |
| Serious register: beans, dose, price, hours | Drama register: sign-offs, captions, notifications |
| The site and its locality | The salutation written to it |

## 0.5 Voice: the two registers

The brand sounds like a familiar barista who knows your order and does not need to make conversation about it. Warm, brief, a little dry, never explaining itself.

| | Serious register | Drama register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Covers | Beans, roast, dose, training, price, hours, hygiene, allergens | Sign-offs, captions, seasonal notes, apologies, anything after the P.S. |
| Sounds like | Flat declaratives, no wordplay, no jokes | Gossipy, brief, complicit, deadpan |
| Example | 18 grams in, 36 out, 27 seconds. Every pod, every shift. | P.S. Someone ordered a quad at 8:40. We are monitoring the situation. |

**The governing rule: dramatic content, deadpan delivery.** The subject matter can be as theatrical as you like. The sentences stay short, flat and unpunctuated by enthusiasm.

- Wrong: Someone ordered a QUAD at 8:40am! Legend!
- Right: P.S. Someone ordered a quad at 8:40. We are monitoring the situation.

The two registers never mix inside one sentence. A dose weight never carries a joke. A sign-off never carries a specification.

## 0.6 Copy rules: non-negotiables

**Always**

- Short sentences. One idea per line.
- The reader is "you", one person, never a demographic.
- State quality facts plainly. Say the grade, never the species.
- Outlets are always Pods, always capitalised.
- P.S. is always written with both full stops.
- One P.S. sign-off per surface. On a web page, read surface as page section, maximum three per page.
- The P.S. is always the last line on its surface, never the headline.
- Let the P.S. carry the personality so the rest can stay functional.
- Report what happened. Let the fact be the interesting thing.

**Never**

- Announce. No launching, introducing, unveiling, we are excited, proud to, thrilled, now available.
- Explain the joke. No "get it, like a postscript."
- Crafted, artisanal, curated, journey, premium, passionate about coffee, gourmet.
- Cheap, affordable, budget, discount, value-for-money, student logic.
- 100% Arabica, or any species claim, anywhere. See 0.8.
- Community, family, tribe.
- Moralise. Nothing about deserving a break or fuelling anyone's hustle.
- Exclamation marks or emoji in brand copy.
- Third person about the brand.
- Apologise for being grab-and-go.
- Mention or imply a competitor, by name or by description.
- A product description longer than two lines.

**The four tests before any P.S. line ships**

1. Could a person say it out loud to a regular without sounding scripted?
2. Does it add something, rather than repeat what is already on the surface?
3. Is it under about ten words?
4. Would an exclamation mark help it? If yes, the line is too weak. If it would ruin it, the line is right.

A fifth test for everything: does it survive being read three hundred times by the same person? This brand is read daily by the same people for years.

## 0.7 The price doctrine

Price operates at three levels and only two are public.

| Level | What it is | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The gap | India fell in love with specialty coffee and never built a daily price point for it. A category observation. | About page, founding narrative. Stated once. |
| The number | What a cup costs. | Menu page and Pass cards, as data. Never explained. |
| The argument | Why the format allows the number. | Investor decks and partner conversations. Never in customer copy. |

**State the gap once. Price the cup plainly. Never argue the case.**

Price never leads at any stage of the customer relationship. At trial, lead with the cup. Once the habit is formed, lead with the range. If the affordability is real, the customer works it out on the second visit, which is the only version of that realisation that sticks.

Affordable, not cheap. Never apologise for the price and never frame it as a bargain.

## 0.8 The quality claim

**The rule: claim the grade, never the species.**

The bean composition is a sourcing decision that must stay free to change. Every Arabica claim from v3 is removed. No copy anywhere states or implies which species are in the cup, and no copy is anti-Robusta.

| Context | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Quality claim, factual | Specialty grade, every lot. |
| Brand and positioning copy | Honest specialty coffee |
| Menu section label | Coffee · Specialty grade · Ground at order |
| Short form, bios and strips | Specialty grade. Every cup. |
| If a blend is used, stated plainly | Blended for the cup, not for the label. |

**Before publishing:** confirm with the roaster that every lot can be documented at specialty grade, and check how Indian labelling rules treat the claim on packaging and menu boards. If it cannot be documented per lot, soften the absolute phrasing.

## 0.9 Placement language

The v3 copy said "inside the buildings you already use" everywhere, which is only true of some Pods.

- Umbrella phrase: **in and around the places your day already runs through**
- Concrete version: **inside your building, or the corner you pass on the way in**
- Never claim every Pod is inside.

## 0.10 Language register

English, with a small number of Hindi and Gujarati words for texture. Words, not sentences. Never code-switch mid-sentence.

Use local words for object and place labels, playlist names, and one or two site-specific product names. Roughly three to five local words live in a Pod. Do not use them in sign-offs, captions, notifications, menu board copy, pricing or allergens.

## 0.11 Vocabulary

| We say | We never say |
| --- | --- |
| Pod | Kiosk, outlet, cafe, store, counter, branch |
| Specialty grade, honest specialty coffee | 100% Arabica, any species claim, premium, artisanal, crafted, curated |
| Honest pricing, honest cup | Cheap, affordable, budget, discount, value-for-money |
| Daily drinker, the everyday cup | Coffee lover, connoisseur, caffeine addict |
| The Usual | Smart favourites, saved order, your regular |
| The Letter, The P.S. | Core menu, specials, seasonal range |
| Ground fresh at every order | Freshly brewed, barista-made |
| Report, observe, land | Introduce, launch, unveil, announce |

## 0.12 Pod naming

Three layers, kept separate.

| Layer | Example | Who uses it | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Code | PS-04 | POS, inventory, rota, financial model, suppliers | Never |
| Name | P.S. Coffee, [locality or building] | Customers, app, maps, delivery, staff | Only if the building is renamed |
| Salutation | Dear Fourth Floor, | Window, opening, socials, site copy | Freely |

**Code.** P.S. as Pod Site. Sequential by opening date, never reused, no segment encoded, city letter only on entering a second city. Never number Pods as P.S., P.P.S., P.P.P.S.

**Name.** Brand first, plain location. Use the words someone would say when giving directions, not the lease name or the developer's branding. Staff say the name, never the code.

**Salutation.** One line per site. *Dear [locality]. We are in your lobby now.* Corporate: *Dear Fourth Floor.* Campus: *Dear Everyone With A 9am.*

**Return address plate.** Small, near the counter, identical format at every site:

```
P.S. COFFEE
PS-04 · Prahlad Nagar
Opened March 2027
Fourth letter.
```

## A3 — Why P.S.

**W1 framing:** We are not trying to be the headline. We are trying to be the P.S.

**W3 narrative:** A postscript is the thing you write after you think the letter is done. The thought that refused to stay quiet. The line that turns out to be the most important one in the whole message. P.S. Coffee is that moment: the cup you almost skipped, the one that changed how the morning felt.

**W3, the letter half:** A postscript needs a letter. The letter is your day: the meetings, the deadlines, the lectures, the commute. We are the line you add after it. Which is why every Pod is written to the building it sits in, and why the last line on everything we make is a P.S.

**The readings, customer-facing.** Three only.

| Reading | Context |
| --- | --- |
| P.S. = Postscript | Core brand storytelling |
| P.S. = Pretty Serious | B2B and corporate |
| P.S. = Plain & Simple | Anti-pretense contexts |

Perfectly Sourced, Pour Slowly, Proudly Specialty, Precisely Steeped and Personal sign-off are retired from customer copy. That wordplay instinct now lives in the object labels at Part E.

## A5 — What P.S. is not

Anti-positioning. Each line stands alone. W2 throughout.

- We are not a cafe. There is nowhere to sit, and that is the point.
- We are not an aesthetic. There is no vibe to photograph. The cup is the experience.
- We are not a treat. A treat is occasional. We are built for every day.
- We are not a machine. A machine serves whoever is nearest. We serve you, specifically, the same way every time.
- We are not a discount brand. The price is low because the overhead is, not because the coffee is.
- We are not a one-version brand. Every drink on our board has more than one way of being made, because a cup you have five times a week should not be a single fixed thing.

***P.S. We are the coffee. Just the coffee. Entirely the coffee.***

**Scope rule for the americano.** It is the clearest illustration of the range and it is not the scope of it. Any line naming it carries the whole board with it, either explicitly or by saying "each drink" instead. Write *there is a right americano for a Monday and a different one for a Thursday, and the same is true of the cappuccino*, never the first half alone.

## A7 — Honest pricing, short form

Published in exactly one place, the About page. Never with numbers, percentages or competitor prices.

| Length | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Compressed (W1) | The price of a coffee. Not the price of a cafe. |
| Full (W3) | A cafe's price carries its room: the seating, the air-conditioning, the address, the floor you occupy one chair of. We built a format where the price only has to carry the cup. We did not lower the bean standard to get there. We removed everything around the bean. That is the whole of the pricing story, and we would rather you noticed it on your second visit than read about it on your first. |

## A8 — The habit philosophy

**W1:** We want to be your habit. Not your obligation.

**W3, the distinction:** A routine is something you do without thinking; it has no value beyond execution. A ritual is something you return to because it gives you something real. The cup you reach for not because it is nearest, but because it is the right one. A postscript is chosen. You could have left the letter as it was. You added the line because it mattered.

**W3, the architecture:** Week one, your first cup exceeds what you expected from a grab-and-go, and that gap is where trust starts. Month three, we have disappeared into your routine. You don't think about us. You just show up. The cup is the brand now, which is exactly what a good habit does.

## A9 — Mission and vision

| | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Mission (W1) | To put a serious cup within ninety seconds of where people actually spend their day. |
| Mission body (W3) | Not a destination. Not a treat. The coffee you have on an ordinary Tuesday, made to a standard that has no business being available on an ordinary Tuesday. |
| Vision (W1) | A working day where good coffee is ordinary, and still worth looking forward to. |
| Vision body (W3) | The first half is achievable by anyone with enough outlets. The second half is the harder one, and it is the one we are actually building for. |

No scale claims, no numbers, no aspirational headcount. Keep the delivery dry: label, statement, one line. No P.S. line in this block.
