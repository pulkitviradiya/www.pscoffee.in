# Corporate food-tech ecosystem: outreach angle

**Written:** 2026-09-22 · **Status:** plan only. Nothing here has been sent to anyone.
**Source research:** [seo-keyword-strategy.md](seo-keyword-strategy.md) §5.

The finding this document acts on: several corporate food-tech platforms already hold the
facilities-manager relationship inside the exact buildings P.S. Coffee wants to place Pods in, and
none of them cover specialty coffee as a category. That is a channel, not a competitor set.

---

## 1. The one-sentence angle

**We are not the cafeteria, and we are not trying to be. We are the coffee counter your cafeteria
vendor does not cover.**

Everything below is that sentence, re-aimed per audience. It works because it is true, it removes
the threat from the conversation in the first line, and it is the same complementary-vendor
framing now live on `partnership.html` and both Tier-2 pages.

---

## 2. Who to approach, in what order

| Tier | Who | Why them, why now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — approach first** | **KhanePe** (a RoomPe company) | The only name in the research that is hyper-local to both target cities: Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar, the same localities, the same buildings, a complementary category. Smallest gap between their footprint and the first Pods |
| **1** | **RoomPe** | Same group. Institutional catering plus student housing. Opens the campus and hostel conversation that the campus Tier-2 page will later need |
| **2 — after a first Pod exists** | **MealPe** | Purpose-built for GCCs, tech parks, universities and co-working. The best structural fit for placement at scale, but a platform partnership is easier to land with one live Pod to point at |
| **2** | **GoKhana** | Same category as MealPe, competing platform. Approach whichever responds; do not run both conversations in parallel without saying so |
| **3 — enterprise, later** | **HungerBox**, **SmartQ** | Large tech parks and campuses, facility-manager dashboards, meal-card integrations. Right for a multi-Pod story, wrong for a pre-launch one |
| **3** | **Pluxee** (Sodexo Benefits), **Sodexo** | Payment acceptance, not placement. Only relevant once Pods have fixed addresses and a POS |
| **Not an outreach target** | **Petpooja** | POS tooling, Ahmedabad-headquartered. A vendor conversation if we ever need POS, not a placement channel |

**Sequencing rule:** Tier 1 now, Tier 2 once one Pod is open and has a week of real numbers,
Tier 3 once there are several. A pre-launch brand approaching an enterprise platform has nothing
to put in the second email.

## 3. What each side actually gets

**What they get.** A category they do not serve, run by someone else, inside a building where
they already hold the relationship. Coffee is the amenity their client's employees complain about
most and the one a cafeteria contract handles worst. They keep the meal relationship untouched.

**What we get.** The introduction. The hard part of Pod placement is not the format or the cup,
it is getting to the facilities manager with someone credible already in the room.

**What neither side should pretend.** This is not a technical integration, a revenue share or an
exclusive. It is a warm introduction and, at most, a co-presented offer. Say that plainly rather
than dressing it as a strategic partnership.

## 4. The approach, written

B2B is the one context where *P.S. = Pretty Serious* belongs (Handbook, channel playbook). Lead
with what removes work from the reader. No positioning line, no brand poetry, no numbers on a
first email.

> **Subject:** The coffee counter your cafeteria contracts do not cover
>
> We are P.S. Coffee, building compact specialty coffee Pods for offices, co-working floors and
> campuses in Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar. Fifty to a hundred square feet, we bring the counter, the
> barista and the beans.
>
> We are not a cafeteria and we are not trying to become one. In the buildings you already serve,
> coffee is usually the gap: a machine in the pantry, or a walk outside. That is the only thing we
> do.
>
> If it is useful, we would rather work behind your relationship than around it. Worth a
> conversation.
>
> P.S. Pretty Serious about this.

**Rules for anyone sending this.** One P.S. line, last. No exclamation marks. No claim that a Pod
is open. No price, no commercials in writing before a call. Never describe the recipient's
category as something we improve on.

## 5. What not to do

- **Do not name any of these companies in visible website copy.** Master Copy v4 bans naming or
  implying another business, and the keyword database marks every Ecosystem row accordingly. The
  discoverability play is category association, not name-dropping.
- **Do not pitch as a replacement.** The moment the email reads as "instead of your vendor", the
  facilities manager forwards it to that vendor and it dies.
- **Do not promise meal-card acceptance.** That is a POS and a fixed address away, and neither
  exists yet.
- **Do not approach two competing platforms in the same building at once.** Both will find out.

## 6. What has to exist before the first email goes out

1. A named sender and a real reply address. These conversations do not survive `hello@`.
2. The partnership page and both Tier-2 pages live, because the first thing a recipient does is
   look the brand up. All three now are.
3. A one-page PDF or link with the format, the footprint and what the host provides. The Tier-2
   pages already carry that content and can serve as the link.
4. Brand-owner sign-off on who sends it. **This document plans the outreach; it does not authorise
   it.**
