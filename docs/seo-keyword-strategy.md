Compiled 2026-09-21, aligned to Master Copy System v4 and the Brand Language Handbook
(see [copy-system.md](copy-system.md)). This is phase 1 — the keyword landscape — before any
content brief, page rewrite, or campaign work. No SEO tool (Ahrefs/Semrush/GSC) is connected to

## 0. A brand-voice tension worth flagging before anything else

The highest-volume real search terms in this category use words the Brand Language Handbook
bans from P.S. Coffee's own copy: *affordable*, *cheap*, *budget*, *near me + price*, and any
species claim (*100% Arabica*). That's not a contradiction to resolve by breaking the voice
rule — **the rule governs what P.S. Coffee says, not what a page can be found for.**

Practical split:
- **Meta titles/H1s/URL slugs** can and should carry the literal high-volume phrase a person
  types (e.g. a page titled to match "affordable specialty coffee Ahmedabad" search intent).
- **The visible body copy on that page** stays in brand voice — "honest pricing," "specialty
  grade, every lot," never "cheap," "affordable," or "100% Arabica" in a sentence.
- This is standard practice even for brands with a strict no-price-comparison voice (Apple's own
  pages don't say "cheap," but Apple's SEO still targets "affordable MacBook" queries elsewhere).
  Flag this explicitly in any content brief so a writer doesn't accidentally violate voice while
  chasing a keyword, or accidentally drop a real keyword to protect voice purity.

This split matters even more for one specific case: the second research pass (see intro) proposed
"P.S. Coffee is a **100% Arabica** specialty coffee brand... **accessible pricing**" as the site's
core search-entity definition. That phrasing predates Master Copy v4 (its own source list cites
the older Brand Bible, not v4 or the Handbook). §1 below gives the corrected version — the species
claim and the affordability framing don't belong in *any* entity statement meant for AI systems to
quote back, because AI citation makes a claim durable in a way a single web page isn't: once an AI
system has learned "P.S. Coffee = 100% Arabica," that association is much harder to walk back than
editing a page.

## 1. The P.S. Pod → "coffee kiosk" translation, and the corrected entity definition

Nobody searches for "P.S. Pod" yet — it's an invented term with zero existing search volume. But
the underlying category ("compact coffee kiosk inside a building") has real, if modest, search
volume. The fix, borrowed from the second research pass and worth acting on: **state the
translation in plain text somewhere on the site**, so search engines and AI systems can map the
proprietary term to the generic category a person actually typed.

> A P.S. Pod is a compact grab-and-go specialty coffee kiosk, placed inside or around offices,
> co-working spaces, campuses and gyms.

That sentence (or a close paraphrase already used in `pods.html`'s "What is a P.S. Pod?" copy)
does real SEO/GEO work: **Pod = coffee kiosk = compact café format = grab-and-go coffee =
workplace coffee solution**, all connected for a machine that doesn't know what "Pod" means yet.

### The corrected entity definition (for anything meant to be quoted back by an AI system)

Not this (the version the second research pass proposed, which predates the current brand voice):

> ~~P.S. Coffee is a 100% Arabica specialty coffee brand built around compact grab-and-go Pods...~~

This instead — grade claim, not species claim; range as the differentiator, not price:

> **P.S. Coffee is a specialty-grade grab-and-go coffee brand built around compact Pods — placed
> in and around the offices, co-working spaces, campuses and gyms where people already spend
> their day — serving the same drink in several ways, because a cup you have every working day
> should be allowed to be interesting.**

Five connected search territories this creates, same structure the second pass proposed, corrected
wording: **coffee consumer → grab & go → Pod / compact format → workplace / campus → B2B
partnership.** This is a stronger, more defensible identity than competing to be "another
Ahmedabad café," and it's one no competitor's content can answer as well, since it's built from
P.S. Coffee's own invented structure (The Letter / The P.S.) rather than a category generality.

## 4. AI-search (GEO/AEO) keyword & structure targets — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, AI Overviews

2026 reality, from research: AI Overviews cut click-through on top Google results by ~58%;
ChatGPT alone processes 2.5B prompts/day, ~65% of which are search-like. Three disciplines now
matter together — **SEO** (rankings), **AEO** (being the extracted answer), **GEO** (being cited
inside a generated answer). None of these are separate keyword sets from §3 — they're the same
demand, surfaced differently, and won via **structure and citability**, not new keywords.

### What actually moves the needle (not more keywords — better answer-shaped content)

1. **FAQPage schema is the single highest-leverage move available right now.** Research found
   FAQ-schema'd pages are dramatically more likely to be cited by AI systems even though Google
   stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026 — the schema still feeds AI extraction. P.S.
   Coffee's blog posts already carry FAQPage JSON-LD (restored in the 2026-09-21 blog rollback) —
   this is already a real advantage over abCoffee/First Coffee/Nothing Before Coffee, none of
   whose fetched pages showed structured FAQ data.
2. **LocalBusiness schema with consistent NAP** (name/address/phone matching Google Business
   Profile exactly) — 73% of local businesses audited in a 2026 study had zero schema; the ones
   with clean schema were 2.7x more likely in the local pack and 3.2x more likely cited in AI
   Overviews. P.S. Coffee is pre-launch with no fixed address yet, but each Pod opening should
   ship LocalBusiness schema on day one, not retrofitted later.
3. **Short, direct, quotable answers (40-60 words) win AI extraction** — matches Master Copy v4's
   own voice rule almost exactly (short sentences, one idea per line, W3 body capped tight). The
   brand voice and AI-extractability are not in tension here; lean into it.
4. **Citable specifics beat vague claims** — one study found adding quotations/statistics to a
   page raised its share of AI-generated answers by ~30-41%. Concrete numbers already exist in
   restored blog content (90-second service claim, 50-200 sq ft footprint) — keep these as
   verifiable facts, not vague "fast and fresh" language, specifically because AI systems weight
   concrete/quotable claims higher.
5. **Crawlability for AI crawlers specifically** — confirm `robots.txt` doesn't block OpenAI's
   (`GPTBot`), Google's AI (`Google-Extended`), or Anthropic's (`ClaudeBot`) crawlers; this is a
   separate check from Googlebot access and easy to get wrong by accident.

### Question-phrasings to write content around (how people actually prompt AI, not just Google)

General/category:
- "Is there a grab-and-go specialty coffee brand in Ahmedabad?"
- "What's a good coffee brand for offices in Gujarat?"
- "What is the difference between a coffee kiosk and a café?"
- "What are alternatives to expensive café coffee?" — answer via the Pod-format explainer, never
  a price-defence piece (see §3's price-doctrine warning)

Ahmedabad / Gandhinagar / GIFT City specific:
- "Where can I find grab-and-go specialty coffee in Ahmedabad?"
- "Where can I get coffee near corporate offices in Ahmedabad?"
- "Which coffee brands have Pods in Ahmedabad?"
- "Where can I get specialty coffee in Gandhinagar or GIFT City?"

Corporate / campus:
- "Which coffee brands set up kiosks inside corporate offices?"
- "How can a company install a coffee kiosk in its office?"
- "How can a coworking space add a specialty coffee counter?"
- "Which coffee brands operate inside university campuses?"
- "What coffee options can replace vending-machine coffee?"

Ecosystem (see §5):
- "Which coffee brands work with HungerBox / MealPe / GoKhana / SmartQ?"
- "What coffee brand can be added to a corporate cafeteria vendor network?"

Brand-specific, once there's enough public surface area:
- "Compare abCoffee vs [P.S. Coffee] vs Nothing Before Coffee" — not yet, but worth planning the
  honest comparison page for when it is (never naming a competitor by name in brand copy per the
  Handbook — but a dedicated "how P.S. Coffee compares" page CAN name competitors factually,
  since that's a different surface with different rules than social/brand copy; confirm with
  brand owner before publishing anything competitor-named, per the Handbook's escalation rule)
- "What is The Letter and The P.S. menu structure?" — once that terminology is live and indexed,
  this is a distinctive, unclaimed AI-answer opportunity precisely because it's P.S. Coffee's own
  invented structure; no competitor content will ever answer it better.

## 5. B2B / corporate / co-working / university collaboration keywords — the "demand but no supply" gap

This is the strongest opportunity in this whole research pass, and the one both independent
research passes converged on without prompting each other. Evidence: multiple corporate food-tech
platforms are **already operating inside the exact buildings** P.S. Coffee wants to place Pods
in — co-working floors, tech parks, GCCs, university campuses, corporate cafeterias — for meals
and catering, but none of them cover specialty coffee as a category. That's a real, evidenced gap.

own vocabulary ("vendor," "amenity," "footprint," "no setup complexity" — already in Master Copy
v4's B7 section) rather than only consumer-brand language, **and** should be discoverable next to
searches for MealPe/GoKhana/HungerBox/SmartQ/KhanePe as a complementary (not competing) category —
i.e., "we don't replace your cafeteria vendor, we're the coffee counter your cafeteria vendor
doesn't cover." Worth an explicit sentence to that effect on the Partners page once this becomes
a content workstream.

## 7. Franchise / location-owner keywords (secondary — a different audience entirely)

Both research passes agree this is a real but *separate* search audience — potential investors or
franchisees, not coffee drinkers or facilities managers — and should stay secondary:

- coffee franchise Gujarat / Ahmedabad / Gandhinagar / Surat / Vadodara
- specialty coffee franchise Gujarat
- grab and go coffee franchise India
- coffee kiosk franchise India / coffee kiosk investment India
- low investment coffee franchise

**Do not let this cluster become the primary SEO identity.** The consumer + workplace + campus
positioning in §1 is the differentiated one; franchise content, if pursued at all, belongs on a
clearly separate page (e.g. an "invest" or "franchise enquiry" page) so it doesn't dilute the
consumer- and B2B-facing pages' focus.

## 8. What NOT to do

Do not build a large volume of generic, undifferentiated pages just to manufacture traffic:

- "Best coffee in [city]" / "Top 10 cafés in [city]"
- "What is specialty coffee?" / "Benefits of coffee" / "Types of coffee" — generic coffee-facts
  content with no P.S. Coffee-specific angle

This kind of content is exactly what "helpful content" systems (Google's, and AI citation
selection alike) increasingly deprioritize — thin, undifferentiated, first-hand-experience-free.
The defensible territory is content that could only come from P.S. Coffee's actual business
model: **how a specialty-coffee Pod gets deployed inside a workplace, campus, or daily routine.**
That's real, first-party information competitors can't replicate by writing a better blog post.
