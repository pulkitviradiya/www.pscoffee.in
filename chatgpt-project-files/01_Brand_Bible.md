# P.S. Coffee Brand Bible

Source of truth for core identity, positioning, voice, colour system, logo usage, and the Pod concept.
Generated from the current P.S. Coffee repo on 2026-07-17.

## Brand

P.S. Coffee is a pre-launch specialty coffee Pod brand for Ahmedabad, Gujarat. It exists to make serious coffee part of an everyday habit without asking customers to pay for the theatre and overhead of a full cafe.

The brand is in the habit business, not only the coffee business. The goal is to become the daily cup people return to because the coffee is good, the price is honest, and the Pod is already where they are.

## Core Positioning

P.S. Coffee takes the specialty cafe and removes everything that is not the coffee: the seating, the large floor plate, the playlist, the table service, and the destination-cafe overhead. What remains is a compact Pod, a serious machine, fresh Arabica, ceremonial-grade matcha, and a price that only has to carry the cup.

## Five Pillars

1. Spot On Quality: 100% Arabica specialty-grade coffee, no Robusta cut, no chicory. Ceremonial-grade matcha in matcha contexts.
2. Spot On Pricing: honest everyday pricing, with Cappuccino starting at INR 89 and the menu range currently INR 89-289.
3. Spot On Reachability: Pods sit inside places people already go: co-working spaces, offices, campuses, gyms, and high-footfall buildings.
4. Spot On Technology: order-ahead logic, favourites, pickup timing, and nearby Pod discovery through the app experience.
5. Spot On Presence: built for every hour awake, from the gym crowd to the morning rush, afternoon slump, and evening matcha.

## The Pod Concept

Always call them Pods, capital P. Do not call them outlets, cafes, kiosks, branches, or stores.

A Pod is the smallest self-sufficient P.S. Coffee unit. It is a complete compact coffee operation in roughly 50-200 sq ft, placed inside an existing destination rather than asking the customer to visit a standalone cafe.

Approved Pod locations:

- Co-working spaces
- Corporate campuses and offices
- Universities and colleges
- Gyms and fitness centres
- Retail or high-footfall daily-use locations

## Meaning Of P.S.

The letters stay fixed. The meaning flexes by context.

- Postscript: the core identity. The thought added after you thought you were done.
- Perfectly Sourced: bean and quality contexts.
- Pour Slowly: craft contexts.
- Proudly Specialty: menu and quality contexts.
- Plain & Simple: anti-pretence copy.
- Pretty Serious: B2B, partnership, and team contexts.
- Precisely Steeped: matcha contexts.
- Personal sign-off: emails, notifications, receipts, and P.S. lines.

Use `P.S.:` for standalone postscript lines in visible copy.

## Voice

Voice principles:

- Direct
- Warm
- Dry-witty only when earned
- Honest
- Confident
- Personal, like a note to one person

The P.S. test: read the line out loud. If it sounds like a brand performing, rewrite it. If it sounds like someone useful and trustworthy talking directly to one person, it is closer.

## Copy Rules

Use English UK.

Do:

- Address the reader as `you`.
- Use `P.S. Coffee`, `P.S. Pass`, `P.S. App`, and `Pods` exactly.
- State quality plainly: `100% Arabica`, `ceremonial-grade matcha`, `ground fresh`.
- Put price beside quality only where approved.
- Keep product descriptions short.
- Use P.S. lines where they add a signature ending.

Do not:

- Use em dashes in visible copy.
- Use exclamation marks in serious copy.
- Use `crafted`, `artisanal`, `curated`, `journey`, `passionate about coffee`, or `premium`.
- Lead general brand, Pod, App, About, Join, Partner, or Blog copy with savings.
- Imply a live Pod, app, service, or availability before launch.

## Copy Hierarchy

- W1: primary brand truth. Hero headlines and the one idea a section must leave behind.
- W2: secondary expression. Context, proof, or a sharper product benefit.
- W3: body. Plain explanation in Space Grotesk.
- W4: microcopy. Labels, CTAs, form guidance, tags, captions, and status text.

A component does not need all four levels. Avoid repeating the same claim at several levels.

## Colour System

Core palette:

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee lead | Electric Terracotta | `#E8400C` |
| Matcha lead | Deep Ceremonial | `#3D6B4A` |
| Main background | Steam Cream | `#FAF6EE` |
| Card background | Oat Off-white | `#F2EBD9` |
| Border | Linen | `#E8DCC8` |
| Terracotta pressed | Brick Deep | `#B8300A` |
| Body text | Espresso Brown | `#5C3518` |
| Heading text | Dark Roast | `#3D2010` |
| Accent | Nectar Gold | `#D98F1F` |

Colour profiles:

- Coffee & Bakery: Terracotta + Steam Cream. This is the everyday face.
- Matcha: Deep Ceremonial + Steam Cream. Reserved for matcha contexts.

Rules:

- Never use pure white, pure black, blue, grey, teal, or cool neutral colours.
- Terracotta and Ceremonial must not touch directly. Use Cream or Oat as a buffer.
- Dark Roast and Espresso Brown are type colours, not large background fills.
- Steam Cream text on Terracotta or Ceremonial is approved.
- Dark Roast or Espresso text on Terracotta or Ceremonial is not approved.
- Nectar is an accent, not a third brand colour. Never use Nectar as a hero fill, nav background, or primary CTA.
- The site-wide footer is always Deep Ceremonial, even on coffee pages.

## Logo Usage

Logo family:

- Primary wordmark
- P.S. monogram
- Circle seal
- Reversed Cream-on-colour marks
- Favicon and app icons
- Square logo
- Rectangle logo

Logo rules:

- Both full stops in `P.S.` are structural. Never remove them.
- Never fuse P, period, S, period into a ligature.
- Do not add a cup, steam, bean, or literal image to the mark.
- Clear space equals the monogram disc height on every side.
- Minimum on-screen UI size: 32px. Favicon may use the monogram at 16px.
- Do not stretch, distort, rotate, recolour off-palette, add gradients, shadows, or effects.
- On photography, put the mark on a solid plate. Do not place it loose over a busy image.

## Approved Signature Lines

- P.S. Your coffee is ready.
- P.S. You deserve this break.
- P.S. Tomorrow starts now.
- P.S. One more coffee?
- P.S. See you in the morning.

For current website copy, prefer colon-based forms such as `P.S.: Your coffee is ready.`
