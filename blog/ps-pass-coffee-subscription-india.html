The short version

The P.S. Pass is a prepaid coffee pass. You choose a pack of cups, pay once, and the balance sits in the app. Every time you order at a Pod, the Pass settles the bill automatically, so there is nothing to pay at the counter. It exists to remove one small decision from your morning, not to act as a discount card. P.S. Coffee is pre-launch, so Pass fulfilment goes live when the first Pods open in Ahmedabad.

What to take away

The Pass is built around friction, not arithmetic. Its job is to remove the 8am payment decision, which is the thing that quietly breaks most daily habits by week two.

You pay once. After that, ordering is three taps and the cup is ready when you arrive. No wallet, no UPI pin, no card at the till.

No auto-renewal without your say. Validity is shown plainly on the pass cards. When the balance runs low, the app tells you, and you top up only if you want to.

We do not sell the Pass as a saving. Each cup is already priced honestly. The Pass just moves the payment out of your way.

Section 01 · The mechanic

How the P.S. Pass will work

Most coffee subscriptions ask you to do three things: pay in advance, remember to use it, and not forget that it expires. The P.S. Pass will ask you to do one thing. Show up.

The mechanic is deliberately plain. You choose a pack that matches how often you drink. You pay for it once. That balance then sits inside the P.S. Coffee app. From that point on, every order pulls from the pack automatically. You open the app, pick your cup, choose a pick-up time, and collect it. The payment already happened, once, on the day you bought the pack.

There is no counter transaction. No fishing for your phone at the till, no UPI pin, no card. For a format built on speed, the payment step is the last piece of friction left in the morning, and the Pass is how we remove it.

Section 02 · The habit

The decision you stop making

Here is the part that sounds minor and is not. Every morning you do not ask yourself “should I buy a coffee today?” is a morning where the habit stays intact.

A daily habit does not usually die from a big moment. It dies from a hundred small decisions, each of which is a chance to say no. The walk-in customer makes that decision every single day: pull out the wallet, tap the card, watch the amount leave. Most people, most days, will still buy the coffee. But the decision itself is a small tax on the habit, and it is the thing that lets week two quietly slide into skipping.

The Pass takes that decision off the table. You made it once, at the start, when you bought the pack. After that the morning has one less thing in it. This is the whole design intent, and it is why we talk about the Pass as a habit tool rather than a loyalty scheme.

The honest bit

By month three, if we have done this right, you will not think about us at all. You will just show up, order, and get on with your day. That is not a failure of the relationship. It is the point of it. A good habit disappears into the routine that holds it.

Section 03 · The terms

What the P.S. Pass is not

Most of the frustration people carry about subscriptions comes from the traps, not the product. So it is worth being clear about what we are not building.

What the Pass will do
  • Settle your order automatically, every time, with nothing to pay at the Pod
  • Tell you when the balance is running low, so you are never caught short
  • Let you top up on your own terms, when you actually want to
  • Show validity plainly on every pack, so you know exactly what you bought
What it will not do
  • Auto-renew and charge you again without your say-so
  • Hide validity terms or make the expiry clock the trick
  • Bury a cancellation button three menus deep
  • Pretend a discount is a reason to commit

We do not auto-renew by default. We do not set traps. A prepaid pass only works over the long run if you choose it every time, so we would rather earn the renewal than enforce it. If a pack runs out and you decide not to top up, you simply go back to walk-in pricing. Nothing is lost, nothing is held hostage.

Section 05 · The pricing contract

Why we won’t sell it on discount

Nearly every subscription you have ever seen leads with the saving. Buy the pack, save fifteen percent, feel clever. We are deliberately not doing that, and the reason matters.

P.S. Coffee is built on one pricing idea: the price of a cup only has to carry the cup, not a cafe. A Pod has no seating, no Wi-Fi bill, no dining room, no air-conditioning across a floor you use one chair of. So the price is already lower than a cafe’s, not because we cut a corner on the bean, but because we removed everything around it. We call that honest pricing.

If the walk-in price is already fair, then a Pass “discount” would be quietly admitting it was not. That is not a game we want to play. Everywhere else, ease costs extra: delivery fees, surge pricing, priority tiers. The easier a thing is, the more you get charged for it. We built the opposite. The Pod is already in your building, the app already knows your order, and the Pass has already settled the bill. Convenience is the product here, not the upsell.
