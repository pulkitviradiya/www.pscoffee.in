The direct answer

Cold brew is coffee steeped in cold or room-temperature water for 12 hours or more, no heat, no espresso machine, no pressure. The slow extraction pulls the flavour compounds that make coffee taste good, while leaving behind the acids and bitter oils that heat releases. The result is naturally smoother, slightly sweeter, and 60–70% lower in acidity than hot-brewed coffee, with no sugar added. It is one of the fastest growing coffee formats in India, and the reason is not complicated: it tastes better to almost everyone who tries it.

Key takeaways

Cold brew is not iced coffee. Iced coffee is hot coffee poured over ice, diluted, more acidic, and flat within minutes. Cold brew is brewed cold from the start, concentrated, stable, and smooth.

The 12-hour cold extraction process pulls less acid and fewer bitter compounds than hot extraction. The cup is naturally sweeter without any additives.

100% Arabica beans make significantly better cold brew. Arabica's lower chlorogenic acid content means less bitterness after cold extraction, Robusta blends produce a flat, harsh cold brew by comparison.

Cold brew is shelf-stable for 7–10 days refrigerated, making it the most practical format for a grab-and-go Pod, no espresso machine required per serving.

India's urban coffee consumers are shifting to cold formats year-round, not just in summer. Rising temperatures, longer working days, and an afternoon coffee habit that a hot latte cannot serve are all driving this.

Section 01 · What it is

What cold brew coffee actually is, and how it is made

Cold brew is defined by its extraction method, not its temperature at the point of serving. You take coarsely ground coffee, ideally 100% Arabica, combine it with cold or room-temperature water at roughly a 1:8 ratio, and leave it to steep for 12 to 24 hours. No heat. No pressure. No machine doing any of the work. Time is the only input.

At the end of the steep, the grounds are filtered out and you are left with a concentrate. That concentrate can be served as-is over ice, diluted slightly with water or milk, or used as the base for other drinks. Because the extraction happens slowly and without heat, the compounds that are extracted are different from what a hot brew or espresso pulls.

Heat is efficient at extracting everything. That includes the flavour compounds you want, sweetness, fruit, chocolate notes, but also the acidic compounds and bitter oils you do not. Cold extraction is selective: it draws the pleasant compounds first and leaves much of the harsh ones behind. The result is a cup that tastes cleaner, rounder, and naturally sweeter, without any sugar or milk required.

Section 02 · Cold brew vs iced coffee

Cold brew vs iced coffee: they are not the same thing

This is the most common confusion, and it matters because the two drinks are fundamentally different in flavour, structure, and behaviour.

Iced coffee is hot coffee poured over ice. You brew it hot, by drip, espresso, or french press, and then chill it immediately by pouring it over ice. The rapid temperature change preserves the acidity and bitterness of the hot extraction. The ice also dilutes the drink as it melts. You end up with something cold, acidic, and progressively weaker as you drink it. The window of when it tastes good is narrow.

Cold brew is brewed cold from the start. The extraction is slow and selective, producing a concentrate with a completely different chemical profile. No ice dilution during brewing. The flavour is stable over hours. It can be served over ice and still hold its structure as the ice melts, because it starts as a concentrate, dilution is actually built into the serving.

Section 03 · Why Arabica matters

Why 100% Arabica makes better cold brew

The bean choice matters more in cold brew than in any other format. Here is why.

Arabica and Robusta differ in their composition of chlorogenic acids, the compounds responsible for the sharp, astringent bite in coffee. Robusta is higher in chlorogenic acid and caffeine, which is why it produces a harsher, more bitter cup even under ideal conditions. Cold extraction cannot compensate for this. In fact, the slow steep tends to emphasise the character of the bean rather than mask it. A Robusta-heavy cold brew comes out flat and harsh. An Arabica cold brew comes out clean, round, and structured.

The flavour notes that Arabica is known for, fruit, chocolate, caramel, are exactly the compounds that cold extraction draws out best. The 12-hour steep is pulling the same notes that a skilled barista would highlight in a hot espresso, but in a format that is gentler on the stomach, cooler in delivery, and more forgiving of the time between brewing and drinking.

This is why we will use 100% Arabica for P.S. Cold Brew. The same standard that applies to our espresso applies here, because the quality logic is identical even if the process is different.

Section 04 · India's cold brew moment

Why India is finally ready for cold brew right now

India's relationship with coffee has always been warm. Literally. Filter coffee in the south, instant in the north, chai everywhere. Cold coffee existed, but it meant a sweetened, milky blended drink served in a tall glass at Cafe Coffee Day. That is a dessert, not a daily coffee habit.

What has changed in the last three to four years is the emergence of a genuinely different urban consumer. They have tried specialty coffee, possibly on a work trip to Bengaluru or on a holiday abroad. They have stopped adding sugar to their coffee. They are drinking one to three cups a day. And they are increasingly spending their working hours in environments, co-working spaces, office parks, gyms, university campuses, where the midday heat makes a hot espresso genuinely unpleasant for half the year.

The temperature problem

Ahmedabad averages above 35°C from March to October. That is eight months of the year where a hot beverage at 2pm is a commitment that most people would rather avoid. The demand for a cold caffeine fix that is not loaded with sugar and milk is not a niche preference, it is a practical need. Cold brew is the answer that specialty coffee has been building towards in this climate.

The afternoon habit gap

India's coffee culture has been primarily a morning habit. Cold brew has the potential to claim the afternoon. The lower acidity means it is gentler on a stomach that has just had lunch. The concentrated format means the caffeine is there when the 2pm focus dip arrives. The cold serving temperature suits the afternoon heat. Cold brew does not replace the morning espresso, it occupies the afternoon slot that hot coffee never properly owned.

Where the search data points

Search volume for "cold brew coffee India" and related queries has grown significantly in the last two years. The interest is not just in the metros, Ahmedabad, Surat, and Vadodara are appearing in the data as cities where the curiosity is translating into actual searches for where to buy cold brew locally. The supply is not keeping up with the demand signal yet. That gap is where P.S. Cold Brew will be when the first Pods open.

Frequently asked questions

Is cold brew stronger than regular coffee?+
Cold brew concentrate is stronger in caffeine than a standard cup of coffee, the long steep extracts a high concentration per volume. But because it is served diluted (either with water, ice, or milk), the final drink's caffeine content is similar to a standard espresso-based drink. The difference is smoothness and acidity, not necessarily caffeine load.
Does cold brew have more caffeine than espresso?+
Cold brew concentrate, undiluted, has more caffeine per ml than espresso. But standard cold brew as served, diluted to drinking strength over ice, is in a similar caffeine range to a double espresso (100–150mg). The exact amount depends on the bean, the steep time, and the dilution ratio used at serving.
Is cold brew better for people with acid reflux or sensitive stomachs?+
Generally yes. The 60–70% reduction in acidity compared to hot-brewed coffee makes cold brew significantly more tolerable for people who find espresso or filter coffee harsh on their stomach. It is not a medical claim, but if hot coffee regularly causes discomfort, cold brew is worth trying as an alternative.
Why does cold brew taste sweeter if there is no sugar?+
Because cold extraction avoids pulling the acidic and bitter compounds that heat releases. When those sharp notes are absent, the natural fruit and caramel compounds in the bean, which were always there, are more perceptible. The cup tastes sweeter not because sweetness was added, but because bitterness was not extracted.

Can I make cold brew at home?+
Yes. Coarsely grind 100% Arabica beans at approximately a 1:8 coffee-to-water ratio. Combine in any container, stir, cover, and refrigerate for 12–18 hours. Strain through a paper filter or fine mesh. The resulting concentrate can be diluted to taste and will keep for up to 10 days refrigerated. The main variable is bean quality, 100% Arabica makes a noticeably better result than any pre-ground blend.
